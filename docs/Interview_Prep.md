# Interview prep

One running file per company, `resume-workspace/prep-<company>.md`, from the first recruiter call to the offer. Claude adds to it after every round, so nothing you learned in round 2 is lost by round 4.

## Start the file when the first interview is booked

Ask Claude: *"Start prep-<company>.md. Use the JD, the resume I sent (from the tracker), the company's careers page and values, and anything I paste."* The file has these sections:

1. **Process:** every stage, done or next, with dates.
2. **People:** each interviewer, their role and background (from LinkedIn, which you paste or open), and anything they said.
3. **Company:** size, product, strategy, culture in their own words, recent news.
4. **The role:** what it really is, in the hiring manager's words once you have them, and what good looks like at 3, 6 and 12 months.
5. **Pay and location:** the band if disclosed, your ask, and the net comparison against your baseline (net against net, base against base, naming the employment route).
6. **Round notes:** after each interview, dictate what was asked and what you answered. Claude writes it up.
7. **Fact check:** every claim you made in the interview checked against `Career_Bank.md`. Anything said that the bank does not support is flagged before the next round, so you do not repeat it with a bigger panel.
8. **Stories:** STAR stories mapped to what the company says it values.
9. **Likely questions:** each one mapped to the story that answers it.
10. **Questions to ask them.**
11. **What to focus on next.**

## STAR stories

Situation, Task, Action, Result, with a number in the result wherever the bank has one. For each story:
- Map it to the company's own stated values or principles, using their exact words.
- Note the honest limits ("no number for this one; do not invent one").
- Note what is yours and what the team or committee did ("the committee's standards cut it," not "I cut it"), without weakening what you did own.
- Keep each answer under two minutes. If time is short, lead with the result.

Have at least these ready: the biggest program you ran; influence without authority; a mistake and what you changed; a time you changed course on evidence; resistance from a whole team and from one person; something that broke and how you fixed it; how you measure success.

## Questions to ask them

Broad fit questions, not niche technical probes. You are checking whether the role, the company and the manager fit you, as much as they are checking you.

- Open with: *"Could you tell me about the role in your own words: what you want it to solve, and what makes it hard?"*
- The role: why it is open now; what the first six months should achieve; what it decides alone and when it escalates; how the team feels about process today (would they welcome this role, or would you need to earn their trust first?).
- The company: where it is heading in the next year or two; the culture in their words; for someone who has been there for years, what has changed and where it is heading culturally and strategically.
- The manager: how they like to work with their team; what they enjoy most and what is hardest.
- Close: *"Is there anything in my background you would like me to go deeper on?"* and *"What are the next steps?"*

Bring 10 or more and never cap the list. When an interviewer already answered one, follow up on their answer instead of skipping it. When the list is revised, questions you kept are never silently dropped.

## Research rules

- **Glassdoor and similar sites:** open every relevant review in full (expand "read more"), closest roles first, and report exactly which pages were covered. A skim of truncated cards misses the details that matter (what the case study was, how long it took).
- **Hiring manager's LinkedIn:** what they built, how long they have been there, and their own words about the team. Their posts about the role are gold.
- **The company's own documents:** values pages, handbook, recent CEO letters. Quote them; interviewers notice their own language.

## Take-home case studies

- Ask the recruiter for the format, the panel and how it is scored.
- The deadline is a window, not a measure of the work. It is designed for people with jobs.
- Before sharing any file with the company, list everything it carries that is not for them (speaker notes, comments, tracked changes, document properties) and send a clean copy.

## After each round

Dictate your notes the same day. Claude adds them to the file, checks your claims against the bank, and updates "what to focus on next." A short thank-you note goes out the same day, naming one specific thing the interviewer said.
